Overview

Signal is a systematic, quantitative framework for assessing the physical and transition climate risks embedded in a global universe of approximately 2,500 publicly listed companies. Scores are expressed on a 0–100 scale, where higher values indicate greater risk exposure. The methodology is updated annually to incorporate new climate science, expanded geospatial data, and revised regulatory landscapes. All scores are asset-level, then aggregated to the entity level using revenue-weighted averaging across operating facilities.

Physical Risk Assessment

  • Asset-level geolocation: Over 320,000 industrial facilities, logistics hubs, and data centres are geocoded using a proprietary database cross-referenced with regulatory filings and satellite imagery. Each facility is assigned a latitude/longitude pair with ±50m precision.
  • Hazard modelling: Seven chronic and acute hazards are modelled — river flood, coastal flood, heat stress, drought, tropical cyclone, extreme precipitation, and wildfire. Hazard intensities are drawn from CMIP6 climate projections under SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5 scenarios through 2050.
  • Damage function calibration: Expected Annual Loss (EAL) is calculated for each asset using industry-standard damage curves sourced from Munich Re, Swiss Re, and the World Bank GFDRR, adjusted for local building codes and infrastructure quality.

Supply Chain Analysis

  • Tier-1 supplier mapping: Primary suppliers are identified through purchasing disclosures, trade data, and AI-assisted corporate communications analysis. Each Tier-1 supplier is scored using the same physical risk methodology applied to the reporting entity.
  • Concentration risk: Geographic and sector concentration indices measure the degree to which a company's supply base amplifies or diversifies its direct physical exposure. High concentration in flood-prone manufacturing corridors (e.g. Mekong Delta, Yangtze Basin) attracts a risk premium.
  • Business continuity weighting: Supplier scores are weighted by estimated revenue dependency, sourced from financial disclosures and industry analyst estimates, then rolled up to a single supply chain risk score per reporting entity.

Management Scoring

  • Disclosure quality: TCFD-aligned disclosures are assessed across 42 criteria spanning governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics & targets. Scores reflect completeness, specificity, and verifiability of disclosures relative to peers in the same sector.
  • Adaptation investment: Capital expenditure earmarked for climate adaptation (flood defences, heat-resistant infrastructure, alternative sourcing) is identified in financial filings and sustainability reports. Higher adaptation investment improves management scores.
  • Engagement history: Board-level engagement on climate risk, science-based target commitments, and third-party audit results are tracked and updated quarterly, sourced from public records and voluntary disclosure platforms (CDP, GRI, SASB).
